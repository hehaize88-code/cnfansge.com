Warehouse decision

Hipobuy QC photos: measurements and inspection checklist.

Hipobuy’s app landing page promotes finding products and viewing QC photos before purchase. Use those images as visual evidence, not as a certification of materials, fit or long-term performance.

Official feature, practical limit

“View QC photos first” helps discovery—but the decision still belongs to the buyer.

Hipobuy publicly positions QC imagery as part of product search. The official pages do not promise that every listing has the same number of images, every angle is covered or every hidden defect can be detected. Save the selected listing and compare it with the exact warehouse record for your order.

Eight-point check

Read the photo set in order

01

Order match

Confirm color, size, version and quantity against the order record.

02

Overall shape

Look for distortion, uneven panels or obvious asymmetry from a straight-on view.

03

Measurements

Compare photographed measurements with a garment you already own—not only a generic size label.

04

Stitching

Zoom into seams, hems, loose threads and alignment around high-stress areas.

05

Color

Remember that warehouse lighting changes color; compare several angles before deciding.

06

Labels & details

Check whether visible labels, hardware and printed details match the selected version.

07

Surface defects

Look for stains, scratches, glue marks, dents or damage from domestic transport.

08

Extra evidence

Request a close-up or measurement photo when the standard set cannot answer a material question.

QC

What photos cannot tell you

A responsible guide separates visible evidence from assumptions.

Do not overclaim from images

  • Exact fabric composition unless a reliable label or test confirms it
  • Comfort, fit or sizing consistency across different sellers
  • Long-term durability after washing or repeated use
  • Whether screen color exactly matches the item in natural light
  • Internal electronics performance, battery health or waterproofing
  • Authenticity or brand authorization

Use a red, yellow and green decision

Red: the wrong option, missing part, clear damage or an unacceptable measurement. Yellow: an important area is hidden, the color may be affected by lighting or the measurement is close to your limit. Green: the visible evidence matches the order and your predefined tolerance. Green still means “no visible reason to reject,” not “guaranteed quality.”

Request another photo when:

A suspected defect is partly hidden, the measurement is missing, or the selected option cannot be confirmed from the available photo set. Name the exact area, angle and measurement points instead of asking only for “more QC.”

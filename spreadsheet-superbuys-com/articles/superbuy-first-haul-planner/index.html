Superbuy First Haul Planner: Items, QC, Weight and Budget

Plan your first Superbuy haul with a practical item worksheet, QC decisions, warehouse deadlines and a complete shipping budget.

QUICK ANSWER

Key takeaways

  • Set a delivered budget before choosing products; the product total is not the parcel total.
  • Keep an item record with the source link, variant, measurements, QC status and warehouse deadline.
  • Compare at least two eligible shipping routes using the packed parcel, not just seller weights.
  • Resolve a wrong item or visible defect before approving international shipment.
01

Start with the delivered budget and a small shortlist

A first haul is a group of purchases sent together as an international parcel. A Superbuy spreadsheet can help you discover products, but it does not decide whether those products fit, remain in stock or belong in the same shipment. Begin with the amount you can spend on the complete delivery. Reserve part of it for Chinese delivery, optional services, international freight and any destination charges before committing the rest to products.

Make a shortlist organized by need rather than by a target parcel weight. An inexpensive extra item is still an extra cost, and a bulky item can change the chargeable volume of the entire parcel. There is no universally cheapest haul size. A small first order can be useful for learning the process, although splitting orders can duplicate shipping starting charges. Compare the total rather than assuming that either small or large is always better.

For each candidate, record why you want it, what would make it unacceptable and how you will check those conditions. A hoodie may need a specific chest width; shoes may require an insole measurement; a collectible may need its original box. This turns a general shopping list into a set of decisions that warehouse evidence can actually support.

  • Separate product spending from the delivery reserve.
  • Remove duplicates and items without a clear purpose.
  • Identify bulky, fragile or potentially restricted products before purchase.
02

Create one item record before you pay

Use these worksheet columns: item name, original listing URL, seller, item identifier, selected color, selected size, quantity, item price, domestic delivery, expected measurement, order number, arrival date, storage deadline and QC decision. Add a notes field for promised accessories or packaging. Keep the original source link even if you discovered the item through a shortened spreadsheet link or a community post.

Open the current listing and verify the selected option. A thumbnail can show a different color or bundle from the one selected in the order form. A low starting price may apply only to a small accessory. Save a screenshot of the option and relevant size chart when ordering. The useful evidence is the current selection, not a recommendation copied from another buyer several months earlier.

Do not combine unrelated costs in a single price column. If seller delivery is shared across several purchases, note that allocation separately. Record the currency alongside each amount, and review the payment conversion shown at checkout. Keeping the original currency prevents a later exchange-rate difference from being mistaken for a change in the seller's product price.

03

Treat warehouse arrival as a decision point

When an item arrives, match its warehouse record to the order number and saved selection. Check quantity, model, color, visible size labels and included parts. Review the available QC photos for the specific questions you wrote down. A distant photograph may show the whole garment but not the measurement or small defect that matters to you. Request a targeted view when the missing information could change the decision.

Give each item a clear status: accepted, needs evidence, return or exchange requested, or excluded from this parcel. Do not use 'looks fine' as a substitute for checking the selected size and variant. Photos cannot establish every property of a product; comfort, long-term durability and hidden construction remain uncertain. Describe only what the evidence shows.

Record each displayed storage deadline. Superbuy's published general guidance states 90 days of free storage after arrival, but your item record and current account terms should determine the practical deadline. Seller after-sales eligibility is a separate clock. Inspect early so that a storage window is not confused with permission to return an item at any time during those 90 days.

  • Match the order and item before inspecting details.
  • Request the missing measurement, not an undefined 'better QC'.
  • Keep unresolved items outside the approved parcel group.
04

Build a parcel estimate with weight and dimensions

Replace seller estimates with warehouse measurements as they become available. Add the likely outer packaging and consider the shapes of the contents. Shoe boxes, rigid bags and protective cartons can create a large parcel even when the items are relatively light. International routes may use actual weight, volumetric weight or another route-specific billing rule. The live route terms determine the divisor, rounding and limits.

Compare a complete consolidation with a sensible split. Consolidation can avoid repeated starting charges, while a split may preserve eligibility for a route or separate fragile goods from heavy ones. A single restricted product can change the available routes for the whole group. Check the characteristics of every item, not just the majority of the parcel.

For each option, save the destination, route name, estimated packed weight, dimensions, displayed freight and selected services. Leave room for measurement differences and any destination charges. An estimate or shipping deposit is not a final carrier invoice. Recheck the completed parcel before accepting a result that materially exceeds your budget.

05

Write packing instructions that do not conflict

State which original boxes must stay, which packaging may be removed, which soft items may be compressed and which contents need rigid protection. Explain the priority if two preferences conflict. 'Remove all packaging' and 'keep the retail box undamaged' cannot both be applied to the same item without clarification. Precise instructions also make it easier to review the packed result.

Optional packing, inspection and protection services should answer a specific need. Read the current scope and charge before selecting them. Removing a shoe box may reduce volume but can increase deformation risk; reinforcement may improve protection but add material. Neither option is automatically correct for every parcel. Keep any service charge in its own budget row.

Before submission, review the recipient details, contents, truthful declarations, route eligibility and protection terms. Use transit estimates as ranges. Avoid arranging a first haul around an event that depends on delivery on one exact date. A low rate is useful only when the route accepts the goods and meets the constraints you actually have.

06

Close the record after delivery

Save the shipping deposit, final settlement, dispatch date, tracking milestones and delivery date. Compare the final packed weight and dimensions with the original estimate. If the difference came from one bulky item, record that instead of dividing the lesson equally across all products. Your own completed parcel is a better planning reference than a screenshot of another buyer's shipment to another country.

Inspect the delivery and retain packaging and evidence if there is a problem that needs a claim. Check the applicable support procedure and deadline rather than assuming that a seller return and a parcel compensation claim are the same process. Record the outcome alongside the original order and QC photographs.

For the next haul, update only the assumptions supported by this record. One successful delivery does not prove that a route is always fast, a seller always reliable or a measurement always accurate. The useful result is a repeatable workflow: discover, verify, inspect, estimate, submit and review. That workflow helps control avoidable errors without promising a particular price or outcome.

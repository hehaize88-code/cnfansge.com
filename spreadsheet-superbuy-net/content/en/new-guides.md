# superbuy-working-days | Superbuy Working Days: Order Processing and Warehouse Timelines | Understand Superbuy processing time, seller dispatch, warehouse checks and shipping milestones with a practical order timeline. | Order timeline

## Start with the stage that is actually waiting
Superbuy working days describe several different processes. An order may be waiting for payment, agent purchasing, a seller, domestic delivery, warehouse inspection, parcel packing or international transport. Those stages have different owners and different starting points. Counting every day from the moment you found a product will not explain which stage is delayed. Start by copying the exact status and the time of its last change into your order record.

The official shopping-agent guide separates purchasing, inspection and storage, parcel submission, payment of international freight, and delivery. Use that sequence to locate your order. A product showing as purchased has not necessarily been dispatched by its seller. A domestic tracking record marked delivered has not necessarily completed warehouse processing. A parcel number created after payment is not, by itself, proof that a carrier has collected the parcel. These distinctions make a support enquiry much more useful.

## Customer-service hours are not a dispatch guarantee
The official Help Center displays online customer-service hours of 09:00–18:00 Beijing time. Beijing uses UTC+8. Those hours describe a contact window; they do not establish a universal purchasing, inspection or shipping deadline. Different teams, sellers and transport providers can follow different operating calendars. Check the notice relevant to your order instead of assuming that every operation follows the customer-service clock.

Convert times using the date as well as the time zone. A message sent late in your local evening may already fall on the following morning in China. Daylight-saving changes in your country can also alter the difference while Beijing time remains unchanged. For a simple record, keep one column in Beijing time and another in your local time. Label both explicitly so a response does not appear late merely because two clocks were compared incorrectly.

## Before purchasing: remove avoidable questions
Create an order brief before payment. Record the current product link, exact option, size, colour, quantity and any seller-specific condition that affects the purchase. If several thumbnails represent different items, identify the intended variation in words as well as by image. A vague note can create a clarification round even when the link opens correctly. The agent cannot reliably infer which variation you meant from an old screenshot.

After submitting, check whether a payment or instruction is still outstanding. Keep the payment reference beside the order number, but do not post private account information in public discussions. If the platform asks you to approve a revised price or domestic shipping charge, the order is waiting for your decision. Record when you answered. That separates a processing delay from time spent waiting for missing information, and avoids repeatedly asking for an update before the next action is possible.

## Seller dispatch and domestic delivery are separate
Once the purchase is confirmed, identify the seller's expected dispatch date. In-stock goods, pre-orders and customised products should not share one assumed timeline. Save any promised date with its source and the exact option ordered. A seller may have stock for one size but not another, so a general listing label is weaker evidence than a response about your specific purchase.

After dispatch, follow the domestic tracking number independently of the international parcel. Note the first carrier scan, the destination warehouse and the delivery event. The Help Center advertises a delivery-reminder function after three days for shopping-agent orders; that is an enquiry option, not a guarantee that every seller will dispatch within three days. Use the available order enquiry when a stated date passes, and ask whether the next action belongs to the seller, carrier or agent.

## Warehouse receipt begins another sequence
A courier delivery event and an item becoming available in your warehouse are different milestones. Matching the parcel to an order, checking the contents, photographing the item and updating the account can take place after the courier scan. Compare the order identifier and tracking number before treating a delivered parcel as missing. If several sellers shipped together, establish which items that tracking record actually covers.

The fee page describes priority stock-in as a separate paid service. If you consider it, read the current eligibility, start time and scope in your account. A faster stock-in option does not accelerate the seller's earlier dispatch or the later international flight. Choose a service because it addresses the stage currently waiting, not because the word priority sounds like a complete end-to-end guarantee.

## Review QC while return options are still available
Once warehouse photos appear, check identity, quantity, visible size and any measurement that matters to your purchase. Use the order brief as the comparison point. If you need another angle, describe the exact view or measurement rather than requesting a generic better photo. Record the request date and the answer so you can distinguish an unanswered question from a completed inspection.

Storage time is not the same as a return deadline. Do not wait for other purchases to arrive before checking a potentially wrong item. Preserve packaging and labels until you have made your decision, and ask about the applicable return terms promptly. A long warehouse allowance can help combine accepted items, but it does not extend every seller's after-sales window. Mark each product accepted, evidence needed or return enquiry before adding it to a parcel group.

## Parcel submission does not start every carrier clock
After selecting warehouse items, compare the routes available for the actual destination, contents, weight and dimensions. Save the selected line and its stated delivery range with a date. Read where that range starts: an estimate tied to carrier acceptance should not be measured from the earlier product purchase. Packing requests, address questions and payment adjustments may still need resolution before dispatch.

Track parcel submission, payment, packing, carrier collection, export movement, customs and final delivery as separate events. A tracking label can exist before the first physical scan. An interval without a new scan may need investigation, but it is not enough to identify the cause. Provide the last visible event and its timestamp when asking for help. This lets the support team investigate the relevant handoff instead of answering a broad question about the entire order.

## Build a timeline without inventing delivery promises
Use a worksheet with these columns: order number, product option, current stage, last confirmed event, Beijing timestamp, responsible party, expected next action, source of the estimate and your next review date. Add the domestic tracking reference before warehouse arrival and the international parcel reference afterwards. Keep those identifiers separate, particularly when several orders are combined into one parcel.

For illustration, suppose an item is purchased on Monday and the seller confirms dispatch on Thursday. The useful checkpoint is Thursday's dispatch event, not a guessed delivery date counted from Monday. If the seller then ships on Wednesday, record the actual event without silently rewriting the original promise. This example demonstrates a tracking method; it is not a prediction of Superbuy's service speed. Your record should preserve both expectations and what actually happened.

## Ask one precise question at each checkpoint
A concise enquiry can state the order number, current status, last update, the promised action and the clarification needed. For example: the seller's stated dispatch date has passed, the order remains purchased, and you need confirmation of stock or a revised dispatch date. Avoid sending several different instructions about the same order at once. Conflicting requests can make it harder to know whether you want to wait, substitute or cancel.

Before placing a deadline-sensitive order, allow time for seller uncertainty, QC decisions, packing and destination processing. Recheck official holiday notices rather than assuming ordinary working days apply during every period. When the purchase is finished, record each actual milestone and the reason for any change. That history can improve your next plan, while remaining evidence about one order rather than a universal processing-time promise.

# superbuy-payment-withdrawal | Superbuy Payments and Balance Withdrawals: Fees and Checks | Compare payment methods, distinguish refunds from balance withdrawals, and reconcile fees before moving money. | Payments and balance

## Separate top-up, order payment, refund and withdrawal
A Superbuy top-up adds funds to an account. Paying an order uses an available payment method or account balance for a particular purchase. A refund reverses an eligible charge or credits an adjustment. A balance withdrawal moves eligible remaining funds out through the supported process. These are different events, even when the same amount appears in more than one screen. Identify the event you need before choosing a payment or support option.

Start a small ledger with the transaction date, reference, currency, amount and associated order or parcel. Keep a separate status for requested, processing and completed. A refund request is not evidence that the receiving bank has credited money. Likewise, a visible account balance does not establish that the entire amount can be withdrawn through every payment method. Read the conditions shown for the actual balance and channel before proceeding.

## Compare methods shown in your own account
Superbuy's official pages list card top-ups and channels including Wise, Alipay and WeChat Pay. Availability can depend on the account, country, currency and transaction. Use the options displayed in the live payment flow rather than a static list copied from a forum. Check the destination currency and whether the quoted amount represents the money sent, the account credit or the payment-provider charge.

Compare methods on the same date and for the same intended account credit. Record the total amount charged by the provider, any stated platform or processing fee, and the resulting usable balance. A method with a smaller advertised fee can still produce a different total because of currency conversion. Do not compare one quote before fees with another quote after fees. Keep screenshots of the quotation for your own records without publishing payment details.

## Use an effective-cost calculation
A useful comparison is home-currency money paid divided by account-currency funds actually credited. This describes your effective exchange cost for that transaction. It does not claim to be an official exchange rate. For example, if a hypothetical payment of 105 units produces 100 units of usable balance, the ratio is 1.05. The extra five units must be understood from the provider's breakdown before deciding whether another channel is cheaper.

Label examples as examples and replace them with the figures displayed in your account. Never assume that a previous payment's fee will remain unchanged for another amount or method. Fixed charges can matter more on small transactions, while percentage charges scale with the amount. Also check your own bank's statement for a separate conversion or international-transaction charge. Keep platform, payment-provider and bank charges in separate columns so a later discrepancy is traceable.

## Budget for the two purchase stages
The official shopping workflow separates product purchasing from international parcel shipping. Funding the product does not automatically pay the later freight bill. Make a first-stage budget for the item, Chinese domestic delivery and selected product services. Then maintain a separate provisional allowance for international freight, packaging and destination charges. Update that allowance when warehouse measurements become available.

Avoid adding more balance simply because a product reference price looks inexpensive. First confirm the exact option and the costs excluded from that reference. If you are researching several products, record which are only candidates and which have been approved for purchase. This helps prevent funds being reserved for abandoned choices. Keep a modest uncertainty allowance based on the actual order rather than treating every available top-up as money that must be spent.

## Investigate a payment before repeating it
When a payment appears incomplete, compare the provider's status with the Superbuy account record. A bank authorisation, a completed payment and an account credit are not interchangeable. Save the transaction reference and timestamp, then check whether an error message asks for a specific action. Repeating the payment before understanding the first attempt can create a second transaction that later needs reconciliation.

Use official support for discrepancies and provide only the information needed to identify the transaction. Explain the amount, currency, reference and visible statuses. Never share a password, full card number or one-time authentication code in a public thread. If the provider shows a reversal, record that separately from an order refund. Keeping the payment attempt distinct from the purchase makes it easier to identify which party can resolve the issue.

## Confirm what a refund actually returns
A product cancellation, an accepted seller return and an international-freight adjustment can create different credits. Read the decision for the specific case. Identify the refunded component, any retained charge and the destination of the credit. A product refund does not automatically reverse a completed service or an unrelated provider fee. The amount credited should be compared with the approved refund, not simply with the total originally paid across several stages.

The shipping guide describes reconciliation between the freight deposit and the final charge. Keep that adjustment under the parcel reference. Do not label it as a product refund or assume it has already reached your bank. If the credit is in your account balance, decide whether to use it for a later eligible payment or investigate the withdrawal process. Preserve the original deposit, final charge and adjustment as three separate entries.

## Prepare a balance-withdrawal request
Before requesting a withdrawal, inspect the balance type, the available withdrawal channel, the amount permitted and any displayed fee or minimum. Confirm whether the process is tied to the original payment method. Do not infer that promotional credit, a coupon and a cash top-up have the same withdrawal conditions. Where the account does not explain eligibility, ask support before submitting a request based on an assumption.

Record the amount requested, the currency expected at the destination, the request reference and the processing information displayed. Check the receiving account details through the official flow. This guide does not supply a universal withdrawal percentage or guaranteed arrival time because those details must be verified for the channel and account. A responsible comparison keeps an unknown field explicitly unconfirmed instead of borrowing a number from another user's case.

## Follow the request through to the receiving account
After submission, distinguish platform processing from provider or bank settlement. A completed platform status can be followed by a separate receiving stage. Keep the date of each event. If the stated processing period has passed, ask for the transfer or refund reference associated with that specific request. Use that reference when the receiving institution needs to investigate, rather than starting another unrelated withdrawal.

Match the final credit to the request currency and amount. Currency conversion can make the home-currency result differ from the original top-up even when the account-currency amount is correct. Record the actual difference, but do not automatically attribute it to a platform fee. Check the breakdown from each party first. If multiple small credits arrive, reconcile their total against the request before concluding that the payment is incomplete.

## Keep a reusable payment worksheet
Use one row per money movement: top-up, order charge, service charge, freight deposit, adjustment, refund or withdrawal. Useful fields include account currency, paid currency, gross amount, fee, net amount, reference, related order, destination and final status. Keep proposed costs separate from completed transactions. Mark anything based on an estimate so it cannot be mistaken for a settled charge later.

Before closing a purchase record, reconcile the remaining balance and all open refunds. Keep evidence until the relevant transaction is complete and any dispute process no longer requires it. For the next order, compare current methods again rather than repeating a choice solely because it worked previously. The purpose of this worksheet is a clear account of money paid, credited and returned, with every fee attached to the transaction that created it.

# superbuy-returns-refunds | Superbuy Returns and Refunds: Warehouse Requests to Final Credit | Prepare a return request, preserve QC evidence, separate refund stages and track the money back to the correct destination. | Returns and refunds

## Identify the order stage before requesting a return
A Superbuy return enquiry begins with the location and status of the goods. An unpaid order, a purchase already placed with the seller, an item in the warehouse and a parcel delivered overseas require different handling. First record the order number, the exact item and option, the current status and the latest timestamp. A general request to refund everything can obscure which part of the transaction you want reviewed.

Keep product returns separate from parcel claims and payment withdrawals. A wrong size visible in warehouse photos is an item-level issue. Damage after international delivery may involve different evidence and a transport claim. A remaining account balance is a payment matter. Categorising the problem correctly helps you ask the right question and avoids assuming that approval in one process automatically approves every other refund.

## Check the applicable terms immediately
Read the return conditions for the exact product and seller before purchase, then check the process displayed in your account when an issue arises. Customised, used, sealed, pre-order and other special goods may have different conditions. Do not treat a long storage allowance as a long return window. Warehouse storage and seller after-sales rights serve different purposes and can run on different clocks.

Public policy pages can describe different services or use wording that needs clarification. This guide therefore does not apply one universal deadline to every order. Ask which deadline applies, what event starts it and whether it uses hours, calendar days or working days. Record the answer with the order. If you discover a problem, raise it promptly instead of waiting to resolve an ambiguity at the very end of a possible window.

## Preserve the product and its packaging
Before a return decision, avoid requesting removal of labels, disposal of packaging or changes that could affect the item's condition. Keep accessories and set components together. If the product is still in the warehouse, make the intended hold or return enquiry clear before you submit the item for international shipping. Once a parcel has left, an item-level correction may become much more complicated.

Document what arrived and what was ordered. Useful evidence includes the selected option, seller description, order confirmation and warehouse photos showing the discrepancy. A photo of a missing accessory should be accompanied by the list of components expected for that exact variation. Do not compare your item with an unrelated listing or a promotional image from another seller. The evidence should let another person understand the mismatch without guessing.

## Turn a QC problem into a precise request
Describe the observable issue rather than making a broad statement that the product is bad. Specify the wrong colour, missing piece, visible damage or measured difference. If a measurement matters, state the measurement points and compare them with the seller's stated dimensions. Lighting, perspective and different measuring methods can otherwise make two images appear more contradictory than they are.

If the existing photos do not resolve the issue, request one targeted additional view before deciding. State the angle, label or measurement needed. Keep the cost of extra inspection separate from the product refund under discussion. A paid photo is useful when it resolves a decision; it does not itself guarantee return eligibility or prove properties that photography cannot establish, such as internal performance or long-term durability.

## Distinguish cancellation from a seller return
If the purchase has not been completed, ask whether cancellation remains possible at the current stage. If the seller has already dispatched, the next step may involve waiting for receipt and reviewing a return process. Do not assume that deleting a saved product or changing a spreadsheet status cancels a real order. Use the official account process and keep its confirmation.

When a return requires seller agreement, record whether approval is pending, approved or refused. Save any conditions concerning address, packaging, shipping payment and the expected refund. Do not independently send goods to an address taken from an old conversation. The handling instructions should belong to the current order and case. If the seller proposes an exchange instead, make clear whether you accept that alternative or still request a refund review.

## Compare the proposed refund with every cost line
Separate the product amount, domestic delivery, return freight, purchasing service and optional services. Identify which components the decision says will be refunded. This avoids comparing a product-only credit with a total that also included transport and completed work. Request clarification when the breakdown is missing, especially if several products share an order or a domestic shipping charge.

For a planning example, suppose an item cost 100 units, domestic shipping cost 10 and an optional service cost 5. A proposed refund of 100 should be recorded as a product refund, with the remaining 15 marked for clarification or as excluded according to the actual decision. These are illustrative numbers, not Superbuy rates or a promised refund outcome. Your worksheet should show the approved components and the evidence for each.

## Track return movement independently of money
A return request, a warehouse dispatch and a seller's receipt are separate milestones. Keep the return tracking reference, the dispatch date and the visible receipt event. An approved request is not proof that the item has reached the seller. A delivered return is not necessarily proof that the seller's refund has been processed. Keep those statuses distinct so follow-up questions address the stage that is still open.

If an exchange is agreed, create a linked record for the replacement rather than overwriting the original item. Record the new option and expected dispatch information. Review the replacement's warehouse evidence when it arrives. Reusing the original acceptance decision would hide the fact that a different physical item was supplied. The same principle applies when only one part of a set is replaced or refunded.

## Reconcile account credit and withdrawal separately
Once a refund is approved, check the amount, currency and destination shown in the account. If a credit appears in the balance, it is not automatically a completed transfer back to the original bank or card. Record the account credit first. A separate withdrawal or payment-provider refund may have its own eligibility and processing steps. Keep its reference connected to the original case without treating the two events as identical.

Compare the final amount received with the approved amount, allowing for explicitly documented fees and currency conversion. If the figures do not reconcile, provide the case number and transaction references when asking for clarification. Avoid opening repeated requests with different totals. One clear ledger showing the original payment, approved refund, account credit and any later withdrawal is easier to investigate than several disconnected screenshots.

## Handle overseas parcel issues with the right evidence
For a parcel already delivered, retain the outer packaging, shipping label and photographs of the contents and any visible damage. Record the delivery date and raise the issue through the relevant after-sales process promptly. Do not dispose of evidence while a carrier or platform may need to inspect it. A seller return and a transport-compensation request can involve different conditions and responsible parties.

Review any insurance or compensation terms attached to the selected route and service. Do not assume that insurance covers every item, every cause or every cost. Record the claimed component, the requested evidence and the case status. If import taxes or carrier handling charges were also paid, those belong to a separate review with the relevant authority or provider; a product refund does not automatically reverse them.

## Close the case with a complete record
A practical return worksheet contains the product option, reason, evidence, applicable deadline, request date, approval, return tracking, seller receipt, approved amount, credit destination and final settlement. Assign one next action and a review date to each open case. Keep unresolved cost lines visible instead of marking the entire case finished as soon as one status changes.

Before closing, confirm whether you received a replacement, an account credit or an external refund, and whether any agreed follow-up remains. Use the result to improve future product research: a clearer size note, an earlier measurement request or better packaging instructions may prevent the same issue. Treat that outcome as evidence about the specific order. It does not establish a universal return entitlement or refund time for every future purchase.
